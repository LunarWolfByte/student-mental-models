#Narrow, more specific questions that emerge from the general thought and observations
*Some questions may assume things that are later disproved; questions may also contradict each other.

##General
> What do students think a “successful” high-school experience looks like?
> Why do students choose particular courses?
> How much do students understand the tradeoffs involved in their schedules?
> How much do peers influence course/activity choices?
> How much does family influence course/activity choices?
> How does previous development influence how students view high school?
> How do students decide when they have done “enough”?
> How do students conceptualize intelligence?
> Does intelligence become a social status, and if so, how significantly?
> Why do some highly capable students specialize while others pursue broad interests?
> To what extent do students infer other people's effort from what they see (visible versus invisible)?
> To what extent do students make efforts/achievements visible?
> How accurate are students' beliefs about what selective colleges require?
> When students pursue many activities, what does each activity actually contribute?
> What happens when students have more interests than they have time?
> How much of students' high-school planning is deliberate versus incremental?

##Acceleration, mastery, and educational sequencing
* Should students be allowed to demonstrate mastery and skip a course even without formal credit?
* What counts as sufficient evidence that a student has actually mastered the prerequisites?
* How often does acceleration produce conceptual gaps?
* Are standardized placement tests measuring mastery or test preparation?
* Does accelerating benefit students differently depending on their prior preparation?
* When is taking a course sequentially more valuable than reaching advanced material sooner?
* Should schools distinguish between credit and mastery?
* How much should students study ahead in order to create future opportunities for acceleration?
* Is studying ahead valuable because of the knowledge itself, because it creates flexibility later, or both?
* At what point does studying ahead become an opportunity cost rather than an advantage?
* Should students optimize their earlier education for optionality later?
